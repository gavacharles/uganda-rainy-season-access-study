When the rains come in Uganda, the hospital moves further away.

We were researching something else: whether historical weather data could help contractors plan construction work around the seasons. While counting the days each year when rain stops earthworks, a thought crossed my mind. If rain stops a grader on a murram road, what does it do to a mother trying to reach a health centre on that same road?

So we mapped it: every 1 km of Uganda, every month, by road and on foot, using OpenStreetMap roads, the Ministry of Health facility list, WorldPop population and 45 years of satellite rainfall.

What we found:

🔹 13% of Ugandans are more than an hour from a hospital in dry weather. In April it's 18%, about 2.3 million more people.

🔹 The burden isn't shared equally. Among the poorest fifth, 23% are more than an hour from a hospital in dry weather and 32% in April. Among the richest fifth it's under 1%, rain or shine.

🔹 On foot, 44% of women aged 15–49 are more than 2 hours from emergency obstetric care (HC IV or hospital) in April. By road it's about 1%.

🔹 In places like Malongo (Mayuge), the share of people more than an hour from a hospital jumps from 59% to 93% in April.

🔹 Specific river crossings matter. A bridge at one ford in Riwo, Bukwo would bring about 7,000 people within an hour of emergency obstetric care on wet days.
